How to read this diagram: A nurse registers the patient and captures a retinal image. The AI analyzes the image and produces a risk score, risk level (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW) and biomarker findings — used for triage and decision support only. HIGH and MEDIUM risk patients wait in the MBBS queue (HIGH cases are served first), while LOW risk patients go to the Nurse waiting room where the nurse reviews the case and routes it: MEDIUM/LOW → MBBS, HIGH → Specialist. If a patient requests a specialist, the nurse (or the MBBS doctor) can send MEDIUM/LOW cases directly to the specialist — HIGH cases in the specialist queue are always served first. The doctor opens the patient panel and reviews the AI assessment, biomarkers and previous visits (the raw image is reserved for the specialist's reading screen), then either prescribes and discharges, or refers to a specialist; the specialist reviews the case on the retinal reading screen, completes an assessment and signs off the prescription. Sign-off completes the visit and discharges the patient; the visit is then archived to history. Administration monitors queues and staff to keep the waiting rooms moving.
